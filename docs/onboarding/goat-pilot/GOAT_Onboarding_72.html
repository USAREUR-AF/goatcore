Verify who issued the onboarding files

Q25I received a `.pem` file and a `.cbor` file. What is each one for, and which one comes first?
Documented guidance

The CA .pem is public and identifies the issuer. The .cbor bundle contains your private key and access token: never send it to anyone; if it was shared, tell the issuer. Run `goat ca add`, compare its fingerprint with the independently supplied value, and run `goat profile init` only if they match. If they differ, remove the CA immediately.

Q70Our partner announced a new CA fingerprint before the old CA expires. How do both sides tell which is current, which comes next, and whether their installed versions support the change?
Release check needed

As of this snapshot, v2 overlap is an accepted design, not built. In v2, `identity.ca_fingerprint` is current and `identity.ca_fingerprint_next` is next. Use v1 unless release notes say v2 is built and both sides selected it in reviewed configuration. An announcement alone binds nothing: read and read back the new fingerprint, then compare the agreement digest. On v1, use a gap rotation and expect data to stop. This is neither the SPIRE CA nor the per-peering IPsec transport CA.

Q72Someone reports that the CA’s private key might have been exposed. Who handles that incident on each side, and why is an ordinary scheduled renewal the wrong procedure?
Context needed

Do not perform this yourself unless you are the designated operator. Each side’s operator acts only on systems they control. Where the suspect partner CA is trusted, the local operator first cuts that partner from the tunnel. Then, in this exact order: (1) remove the partner trust domain from the identity service, so refresh cannot restore the CA; (2) remove the partner CA from the edge-router configuration and re-apply it; (3) restart the edge router; and (4) remove the core peering entry and partner store through the reviewed backout process. If your CA is suspect, the counterpart’s operator performs that containment for your CA while your CA operator starts the immediate gap rotation and any site-wide CA response. Counterpart notification is also required by local incident policy, but these cited sources do not say who sends it, when, or through which verified channel; ask a mentor for that procedure, and do not delay the immediate tunnel cut. There is no certificate revocation list: cutting the tunnel is the immediate control, and a possibly compromised CA is never overlapped.
